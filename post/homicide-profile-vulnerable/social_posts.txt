Most homicides happen in two circumstances: domestic abuse and non-domestic brawls. But what about child homicide, elder abuse and gang homicide? This thread summarises what we know about homicide characteristics.

DOMESTIC HOMICIDE

Domestic abuse is a really important factor in homicide: 69% of women and 13% of men who were homicide victims were killed by a current/ex-partner or by a family member. [188 chrs]

----

Some ethnic groups were at much higher risk from domestic homicide than others. While a majority of victims of domestic homicide were White women, Black men and women as well as Asian women had higher homicide rates per million people. [235 chrs]

----

Female domestic-homicide victims were more likely to be killed by a current/ex-partner (74% of such victims) than by a family member (26%). For male domestic-homicide victims the opposite was true: 70% were killed by a family member, 30% by a partner. [251 chrs]

----

For both male and female victims who were killed by a partner, more than three quarters were killed by their current partner and less than a quarter by an ex-partner. [166 chrs]

----

This data suggests that efforts to protect women from homicide should primarily focus on protecting them from being killed at home by their current partners. [157 chrs]

----

Among people killed by family members, the most common circumstance was a person being killed by one of their own children (especially for female victims). Victims killed by family members had a median age of 61, compared to 37 for homicide victims as a whole. [260 chrs]

----

CHILD HOMICIDE

About 69 under 18s were homicide victims each year (75% of them boys). These fall into two groups: under 12s killed by family members and over 12s killed by acquaintances/strangers outside the home. [214 chrs]

----

Very young children were most at risk of being killed by family members: 36% of under 12s killed by family members were under 1 year old and 81% under 5. [153 chrs]

----

95% of children under 12 killed by a family member were killed in a dwelling and 97% were killed by one of their own parents or step-parents, typically a parent the victim lived with. [183 chrs]

----

Protecting young childen from homicide is largely a question of protecting them from their own parents. This is obviously very difficult because parents are also the people who primarily care for almost all young children. [222 chrs]

----

ELDER-ABUSE HOMICIDE

Each year there were about 80 homicides of victims aged 65+, making up 13% of all homicides. Unlike younger victims, victims over 65 were equally likely to be male or female. [196 chrs]

----

Homicides of over 65s were much more likely to be committed by a family member (38% of older victims, compared to 9% of other adults). Of victims aged 65+, 21% were killed by their own child or step-child, compared to only 3% of adult victims aged under 65. [257 chrs]

----

So just as protecting very-young children from homicide is often a question of protecting them from their own parents, protecting older people is often a question of protecting then from their adult children. [208 chrs]
